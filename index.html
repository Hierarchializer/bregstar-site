Questions

Fair enough

What do my clients actually pay for?

An answered phone that books the job. Home-services shops pay $299–$899 a month for it, flat, no per-minute meter. The whole pitch — and the arithmetic of one recovered water-heater job — is on the contractors page. That's the page your clients would see, with your name on it.

What does it cost me?

A platform fee per client you have live, plus a setup for building your agency. The numbers depend on your market and how you want to structure it, and I go through them one-to-one rather than on a web page. You set your own retail pricing; the majority of every dollar your clients pay stays with you.

Do I need to be technical?

No. You never touch a prompt, a model, or a server. You need to be able to explain call forwarding to a plumber and fill in a questionnaire about their business. We handle everything below that line.

What's under the hood?

Our own pipeline — telephony, speech recognition, a frontier language model, and a natural voice — running on our servers, not a reseller wrapper. That's why the margins work and why we control what the assistant says. Every prompt change runs through an automated suite of test conversations before it ships.

Is this a franchise?

You operate under your own brand, set your own prices, and run your own business. What you're getting from Bregstar is the software platform, the setup, and support. The agreement is straightforward and we go through it together before anything is signed.

Why not just do this yourself with an AI tool?

You can rent a voice-agent API by the minute today. What you can't rent is a year of tuning what it says to a customer whose basement is flooding, a compliance posture that survives a lawyer looking at it, and a sales kit that's been used on real contractors. That's what you're buying.
