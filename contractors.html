What it actually does

Booking, not message-taking

An answering service writes down that someone wanted an appointment. By the time you call back, they've booked with whoever answered second.

Books against your real calendar
Checks actual availability, respects job duration and drive buffers, writes the appointment into your Google Calendar, and texts the customer a confirmation. Jobber and Housecall Pro are next.
Triages emergencies
Gas smell, flooding, sewage backing up: it gives the one instruction that limits the damage — find the shutoff, turn it clockwise — then rings your on-call cell and texts you the address.
Answers from your own information
Your hours, your service area, your diagnostic fee, your license number. If it doesn't know, it takes a message instead of guessing.
Hands off to a human, instantly
Anyone who asks for a person gets one — your office during the day, your on-call phone after hours. No deflection, no retention script.
Screens the junk
Robodialers and SEO pitches get nine seconds and a disconnect. You see the count, not the calls.
Says what it is
Every call opens with the business name, that it's an AI assistant, and the recording notice. No fake names, no pretending.
Every call, on the record
Recording, transcript, and exactly what it did — searchable, in 30 seconds, the first time a customer says "that's not what I was told."
A Monday morning report
Calls answered, appointments booked, emergencies dispatched, and the dollar value of the work that came in overnight.

How to start without risking a customer

Put it on your after-hours line first

It can't lose you a call it never gets.

For the first two weeks it only picks up what already goes to voicemail — forward-on-no-answer, nights and weekends. You keep answering your days exactly the way you do now. If it books you nothing, you've lost nothing and you turn it off from your phone.

  1. 15 minutes on the phone with me. Hours, services, pricing rules, service area, where emergencies go.
  2. You forward your line on no-answer. One setting with your carrier — I'll walk you through it while we're on the call.
  3. Two weeks. You get a text after every call it takes.
  4. You decide. Keep it and turn it on for daytime overflow too, or forward the line back. No card until you say so.

The questions everyone asks

Fair enough

My customers will hate talking to a robot.

Some will. They hate voicemail more — most people who reach a voicemail box on a service call simply hang up and dial the next listing. And anyone who asks for a person gets one in about two seconds. Call the demo line and ask for a human; time it.

I already pay an answering service.

Ask them what happens when someone wants Tuesday at two. They write it down and you call back — often after that customer has already booked elsewhere. This books the job on the spot, and it's usually less than what you're paying for the message pad.

What if it says something stupid to my customer?

It's built not to guess. Prices, policies and availability come from your own information or it doesn't say them — it takes a message instead. Every call is recorded and transcribed, and you get a summary by text, so you'll know before your customer does.

Does it tell people it's an AI?

Yes, in the first sentence, along with the recording notice. That's required in a growing number of states and it's the right way to run it anyway. It also never pretends to have a name or to be a person on your staff.

I answer my own phone.

You do — and you answered this one from a crawlspace. The question is the 8pm call, the Sunday call, and the call that comes in while you're under a sink with both hands wet.

What happens to my customers' call recordings?

Transcripts and recordings are kept 90 days by default, deleted on request, and never used to train anyone's model. You can set a shorter retention if you'd rather. Details are in our privacy policy.
